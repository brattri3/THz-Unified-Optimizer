Terahertz wire-grid polarizers with micrometer-pitch Al gratings

Received October 24, 2008; accepted November 30, 2008; posted December 15, 2008 (Doc. ID 103206); published January 26, 2009 We fabricated a terahertz wire-grid polarizer consisting of a micrometer-pitch Al grating on a Si substrate by photolithography and wet etching. The ratio of TM and TE transmittances (extinction ratio) was over 35 dB at 0.5 THz. At the Brewster angle of the Si substrate, the polarization transmittance of a TM wave through the fabricated polarizer exceeded 95% and the extinction ratio was over 45 dB at 1 THz. The fabricated polarizer has a higher extinction ratio than conventional free-standing terahertz wire-grid polarizers. © 2009 Optical Society of America

Recently, with the development of terahertz technologies, such as terahertz time-domain spectroscopy (THz-TDS) [1,2], terahertz imaging [3], and terahertz liquid-crystal devices [4,5], the demand for polarizers is increasing. Wire-grid polarizers and liquid-crystal polarizers are widely used in the terahertz region [6­10]. Liquid-crystal polarizers have a high extinction ratio; however, their working frequency range is
limited between 0.2 and 1.0 THz [6]. Free-standing wire-grid polarizers behave as low-loss elements as the wire grid is unsupported without a substrate [7]; however, the fabrication of free-standing terahertz wire-grid polarizers with a uniform period, fine pitch, and high extinction ratio by using winding metallic wires is difficult [8]. The inkjet printing method is a simple method that can be used to fabricate wire-grid polarizers [9]; however, the grating period 100 m and grating height are limited by the fabrication process.
The properties of wire-grid polarizers are governed by parameters, such as the width of the wire, complex refractive index of the wire, and period of the wire grid. Wire-grid polarizers are required to have a narrow and uniform pitch to achieve a high extinction ratio in the terahertz region. In this Letter, we fabricated a micrometer-pitch metal grating on a Si substrate by photolithography to enhance polarization functions in the terahertz region.
We optimized the wire-grid polarizer using the rigorous coupled-wave analysis theory (RCWA) [11,12]. The key parameters that determine the performance (e.g., extinction ratio) of a wire-grid polarizer are the period, linewidth, and depth of the grid elements. We selected Al as the wire material, since it has a lower resistivity 2.67 10-8 m that leads to a higher extinction coefficient in the terahertz region as compared to those of tungsten 5.4 10-8 m, nickel 6.9 10-8 m, chromium 13.2 10-8 m, etc. [13]. In addition to the grating period, the fill factor of an Al grating is also an important parameter. For gratings with the same period, we examined the effect of varying the fill factor of an Al grating with a period of 3 m. Figure 2(b) shows the transmission spectra as a function of frequency for the variation in the fill factor from 0.3 to 0.5. As the fill factor increased, TE transmittance decreased. The Si substrate had a high reflection loss owing to the high refractive index, and TM transmittance was approximately 50%; however, it should be noted that the Si substrate has sufficient chemical stability, thermal durability, and high robustness. We fabricated a terahertz wire-grid polarizer consisting of an Al grating and a Si substrate. The characteristics of the Si substrate were as follows: 111 orientation, n type, 1455 cm resistivity, 600 m thickness, and a 20 mm 20 mm surface area. By using a phase mask, photolithography was carried out for generating the micrometer-pitch grating structure. An Al film was deposited on the Si substrate by a sputtering method. Then, a photoresist was coated on the film through the phase mask and exposed to ultraviolet radiation generated by a Hg lamp. The exposed resist was rinsed off. Al was etched off by dipping it into an Al etchant using photoresist as a mask. The residual photoresist was removed by dipping it into acetone. Figure 3 shows a scanning electron micrograph of the fabricated element. As observed in Fig. 3, the thickness, period, and fill factor of the wire grid layer were dW = 300 nm,  = 3 m, and f  0.5, respectively. The black spots observed in Fig. 3 were attributed to the oxidization by the etchant.

The transmittance of the fabricated element was measured by using a THz-TDS system in a frequency range of 0.5­ 3 THz [1,15]. Figure 4 shows the transmission spectra of the fabricated element for TM and TE polarizations at normal incidence. By truncating the time-domain waveforms before the multiple reflection pulses penetrated the Si substrate, etalon oscillation was eliminated from the transmission waveform. In TE polarization, transmittance was low 10-2 % ­ 10-1 % . A TM transmittance of approximately 50% was obtained in the 0.5­ 3 THz range, which was the same as that predicted from the Fresnel transmission of the Si substrate. The measured extinction ratio was greater than 23 dB in the broad frequency range of 0.5­ 3 THz. A high extinction ratio of 35 dB was obtained at 0.5 THz, and this result agreed well with the simulation results as shown in Fig. 2.
To further enhance TM transmittance, the transmission spectra of the element were measured by varying the incident angle from 0° to 74° with respect to the grating direction (Fig. 5). According to the Fresnel reflection theory, the Brewster angle of a Si substrate is 74° [16]. As the incident angle was increased, TM transmittance increased. In summary, the fabrication of a terahertz wire grid polarizer with high transmittance and a high extinction ratio was demonstrated. An Al grating with a fill factor of 0.5, a depth of 300 nm, and a period of 3 m was formed on a Si surface by photolithography and wet etching. At the Brewster angle, the transmittance in the case of TM polarization exceeded 95% and the extinction ratio reached 45 dB at 1 THz.
